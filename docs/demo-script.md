# Demo video script (target: 90–120 seconds)

Record the phone screen (the phone's built-in screen recorder works) plus a second phone or the Telegram desktop
app for the family side. Keep the narration at a calm pace.

## Scenes

| Time | On screen | Narration line |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | [PERSON] (photo or clip) holding the phone | 1 |
| 0:10–0:30 | Phone locked and muted; the alarm rings and Couchy opens over the lock screen | 2 |
| 0:30–0:45 | Dose screen: the companion's text and voice naming the medicine; tap "I took it" | 3 |
| 0:45–1:05 | "Tell Grace": say "I already took it, but I feel a bit dizzy"; the reply plays | 4 |
| 1:05–1:25 | Tap "I need help" and speak; show the Telegram alerts arriving on the family phone | 5 |
| 1:25–1:45 | Caregiver settings: the shared QR code, the alarm checklist; the four companions | 6 |
| 1:45–2:00 | Title card with the repo link | 7 |

## Narration (paste into ElevenLabs; ~190 words, use any of the companion voices)

1. This is [PERSON]. Remembering her pills used to depend on other people calling to remind her.
2. So I built Couchy. It rings like a real alarm clock, even when the phone is on silent, even when it is locked.
3. A companion she chose says the name of her medicine out loud, in a warm voice, until she answers.
4. She can simply talk to it. Gemma, an open model, understands what she says, marks the dose, and notices when
   something sounds worrying.
5. If she ever needs help, one button sends her family a voice message on Telegram, right away.
6. Her family connects with a single code, and gets alerts if a dose is late or if there has been no activity.
   It is built with open models, so the same code can run on a laptop, with her data on her own server.
7. Couchy. Built for [PERSON]. The code is open source.

## Tips

- Record the alarm scene first: lock the phone, set a dose for two minutes ahead, turn the volume down and put
  the phone on silent so the audience sees it still rings.
- Show the Render URL or the Telegram bot name once so judges can see it is real.
- Export under 2 minutes and upload to YouTube (unlisted is fine) to embed it in the post.
